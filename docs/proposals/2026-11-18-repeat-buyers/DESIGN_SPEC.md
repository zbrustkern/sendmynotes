# Repeat buyers and observed customer value

**Build handoff · November 18, 2026 · SendMyNotes admin**

A person buys a card for Grandma in November and returns in February for a birthday. Both orders should contribute to the value of the buyer acquired in November, including when the second visit is direct. The founder needs to know how often this happens, how much contribution it generates, and when that contribution covers acquisition cost.

## First release

Add a read-only **Repeat buyers** view under admin Measurement. Start with all independent SendMyNotes sales and allow filtering by first-purchase month and acquisition campaign. Include an explicit Unknown campaign group. Default to all available history so seven independent orders can be reviewed together. The existing checkout, advertising events, and email enrollment need no changes for this report.

Above the table, show the observation date and the earliest covered purchase date. List verified independent paid orders, orders eligible for identity matching, distinct matched buyers, and matched buyers who purchased again. Display counts beside percentages. With small cohorts, “1 of 4” communicates more than “25%.”

| Cohort measure | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Acquired buyer | One matchable sender whose earliest verified, independent, live positive capture falls in the selected cohort. Inspect earlier available history before applying the cohort or campaign filter. Mark incomplete history and withhold new-buyer metrics when prior purchases cannot be ruled out. |
| Repeat buyer by day N | An acquired buyer with a later distinct independent order with positive net payment by that age. Count each buyer once per horizon. Sort by capture time with stable order-ID tie-breaking. |
| Observed repeat rate | Repeat buyers divided by buyers who have reached age N as of the report cutoff. Show 30, 60, 90, 180 and 365 days. Show “Not mature” when nobody has reached the horizon. Missing identity remains visible in coverage. |
| Days to second purchase | Elapsed days between first qualifying capture and next retained paid order. Label any median “among repeat buyers” and include the count. |
| Net revenue per acquired buyer | Start with collected amounts after discounts, exclude pass-through tax, then deduct refunds and lost disputes without double-counting adjustments to the same funds. Divide by all mature matched buyers, including those with no second purchase. |
| Observed contribution per buyer | Net revenue less actual payment fees, landed supplier cost including postage, resends and other documented variable costs, divided by the same buyers. Return an unavailable value and show missing-cost coverage until the required costs are known. |
| Fully loaded CAC and payback | Aligned gross campaign spend plus attributable creative/partner fees divided by verified new buyers. Compare with cumulative contribution per buyer. Show the first observed horizon that covers CAC. Label promotional credits separately as cash savings. |

## Which orders and senders count

1. Start with live capture evidence bound to one parent order. Reuse the admin measurement reconciliation for founder tests, comps, admin orders, duplicate payment facts and unresolved records. Apply explicit commercial exclusions before identity matching. Keep friends-and-family sales outside the independent cohort. If the current reconciliation schema cannot express that exclusion, add an explicit classification and preserve its evidence. Unreviewed eligible captures whose independence is in doubt belong in a visible unresolved count. The November 18 founder count of seven is a reconciliation reference. Nine technically eligible snapshots and five lifetime Google Ads conversions are different populations.
2. Use the **sender’s checkout contact email** where its use for this purpose is supported by the applicable disclosure and choices. Guest checkout is supported. Resolve the effective order email with the existing contact policy, including legacy fallback, synced choice, explicit omission and redaction. Exclude placeholders and restricted records. Review the receipt-only checkout wording and privacy notice before enabling historical email matching in production. If a class of historical records cannot be used for this purpose, report its coverage gap.
3. The existing application trims and lowercases a supplied email and can derive a versioned server-side HMAC. Build on that mechanism. Use a consistent reporting key version for all authorized rows within a read. Use a saved key only when its purpose, version and contact state are compatible. Treat unbridgeable versions as unresolved. Never merge on a partial match, remove plus tags or Gmail dots, combine two addresses by name or mailing address, or use a recipient’s identity. One shared email is one observable sender identity. People using multiple emails can appear as multiple buyers.
4. Anchor a buyer to the first qualifying capture and its recorded acquisition context. A later refund keeps that acquisition and its spend in the denominator. Full refunds remove retained order value and repeat-order qualification. Partial refunds reduce revenue once. Include unrecovered fees and incurred fulfillment costs even for a refunded order. Use event timestamps for historical as-of reads, and label unavailable refund timing or uncertain dispute outcomes. Preserve potentially negative contribution.
5. One charged multi-send checkout is one parent order regardless of recipient count, fulfillment rows, webhook retries or receipt reloads. A second separately paid wedding batch can be another order on the same project. Show same-project reorders separately when a reliable project ID exists before claiming a second occasion. Keep referrals with different sender identities distinct.
6. Freeze the first purchase’s acquisition campaign using the existing measurement attribution precedence. A later direct or paid visit adds contribution to the original buyer cohort. Measure paid reacquisition separately and charge it once where identifiable. Keep unattributed purchases available under Unknown. Keep DocketStamp spend outside SendMyNotes CAC despite the shared Ads billing account.

## Cohort and cost alignment

Use America/Chicago for month labels and report dates, and compare stored timestamps for elapsed ages. For horizon N include the first purchase and all qualifying events through age N, inclusive. A buyer one instant short of N days is still immature. Use financial facts known by the as-of cutoff and define how late-posted adjustments restate a previously displayed result. Display fully refunded, open dispute, missing cost and unresolved identity counts alongside the relevant results.

Only show campaign CAC for a complete, aligned acquisition period. Use the full paid acquisition spend for that campaign period, including clicks that never converted. If critical spend, buyer identity, independent classification, or the attribution window is incomplete, keep the spend visible and return CAC/payback as unavailable or explicitly provisional. For a partly mature cohort, show observed retention for its mature members. Enable the campaign-level payback comparison after every included new buyer has reached that horizon, or where an explicit allocation supports that mature subset. Never divide the whole campaign’s spend by whichever buyers happened to mature first.

## Implementation path

- Add an aggregate-only authenticated GET under admin and a linked view within Measurement. Reuse the existing authorization, environment isolation, query bounds, read coverage, no-store responses and display patterns. Ordinary report reads must be side-effect free.
- Introduce a small, pure cohort calculator accepting sanitized sender keys, deduplicated commercial facts, acquisition tags, dated costs and an explicit as-of timestamp. The server-side source adapter handles identity resolution and restricts Firestore projections. Return aggregate rows, coverage counts and missing-data reasons to the browser. Raw emails, sender keys, click IDs, order IDs, note content and mailing addresses stay out of the report response, URLs, analytics and logs.
- At current volume, compute from authoritative existing order records on demand. Apply a bounded, consistent historical read. Timeouts, truncation, unsupported environment or missing identity-key configuration must return a visible partial or unavailable state. A truncated lookback cannot establish a first purchase. This avoids building a second customer directory or writing keys back to old orders. If volume later requires materialization, make that a separate decision with deletion, replay and key-rotation support.
- Reuse the current data-deletion and preference semantics. Recompute from surviving eligible records. Respect restrictions on campaign attribution and preserve unknown attribution when needed. Keep marketing opt-in separate from internal measurement, and keep the report independent of post-purchase email enrollment, outbound messages and ad-platform uploads.

## Synthetic acceptance examples

- **Clean cohort.** Four identifiable independent buyers were acquired in one fully observed campaign period for $80, including non-converting spend. By day 30, one returned once. Five $9 orders produced $45 net revenue and $25 in actual variable costs. Expect 1 of 4 repeat buyers, 1.25 orders per buyer, $11.25 net revenue per buyer, $5 contribution per buyer, $20 CAC and $15 per buyer still unrecovered. A later direct purchase retains its original acquisition cohort.
- **Immature cohort.** Three of four buyers are 30 days old. One of those three repeated. Show 1 of 3 at day 30. If no buyer is 60 days old, show “Not mature” at day 60. Hold full-cohort day-30 payback until spend and buyer coverage align.
- **Identity and exclusions.** Casing and outer whitespace match the same guest. An omitted email, redacted order, recipient email, unsupported key version, founder test, comp and unresolved friends-and-family record cannot create a new identified buyer. Test guests separately from account holders. An earlier purchase outside the reporting window prevents a returning buyer from being counted as new.
- **Adjustments and retries.** A duplicated capture and ten recipients produce one order. A partial refund reduces revenue once. A fully refunded second order ceases to qualify as a retained repeat. A later refund of the first order leaves the acquired buyer and incurred costs in the cohort. Unknown supplier cost leaves repeat rate available and contribution/payback unavailable.
- **Access and launch.** Verify unauthorized rejection, aggregate-only output, incomplete-read behavior, as-of reproducibility, privacy erasure and guest checkout behavior using synthetic fixtures. No live purchase or fulfillment is needed. A staging preview plus a comparison to manually reconciled aggregate counts is sufficient for founder review.
