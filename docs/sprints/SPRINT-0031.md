# Sprint 0031 — Wedding thank-you projects on staging

**Status:** Proposed for validator review. **Delivery target:** Hosted SendMyNotes staging, including Stripe test checkout. **Planning baseline:** November 18, 2026, staging source `4e076108016687e4ffe17fb4943623eafa98f5ed`. The wedding URL returned 404 during planning.

## Pyramid Index

- **L0.** A couple brings a photo and a guest or gift list, makes a card in their own words, personalizes as many households as they choose, and purchases the ready envelopes in batches they can resume.
- **L1.** Two uploads serve different jobs. Each household has its own envelope, inside greeting and approved final text. Project-wide volume pricing follows purchased cards across batches. Completion requires a discoverable hosted flow, private recovery and a reconciled test payment.
- **L2.** [Sender experience](#sender-experience) · [Note modes and approval](#note-modes-and-approval) · [Project and checkout contracts](#project-and-checkout-contracts) · [Implementation sequence](#implementation-sequence) · [Validator acceptance](#validator-acceptance) · [Release evidence](#release-evidence).

## Starting point and scope

The local 100-household prototype uses fictional rows, browser memory and an estimated price. Staging has a card builder, authenticated and grant-controlled Upload Studio, address-book import, note composition, and a multi-send checkout for 2–25 recipients with one shared body. The existing staged checkout also has retry protection and a review-only fulfillment hold. The sprint must connect those foundations into a real project from import through test payment. A screenshot, a standalone HTML file or an HTTP 200 on a sample screen is insufficient evidence.

Build the wedding occasion first inside a reusable thank-you project. Entry points are a visible **Thank-you notes** action on the staging homepage and general sending surface, `/thank-you`, and a direct `/thank-you/wedding` landing with **Start my thank-yous** and **Try a sample list**. Direct wedding links skip the occasion selector. Preserve attribution and existing drafts. Keep ordinary single thank-you cards and the existing **Customize** action accessible. A navigation click has its own event and never records a cover selection.

The proposed domestic pilot supports **250 draft households per project and 1–100 cards per checkout**. One envelope can thank several named guests. Two people at one address can receive separate cards. A project with 101 ready households can buy 100, then one, with its price history intact. Validate these limits on staging. The existing general multi-send limit remains 2–25. Follow the [project brief](../CANONICAL_PROJECT_BRIEF.md) and [product specifications](../CANONICAL_PRODUCT_SPECS.md) for sender identity, real-pen writing, 17:22 artwork and delivery estimates.

Make private address-request links household-specific, expiring and revocable. Recipients can submit their address only. The sender reviews the change and selects the card for a later purchase.

## Note modes and approval

**One project, mixed use.** Alex writes “Thank you for thinking of us on our wedding day. We’re so glad to have you in our lives.” The first card opens with “Dear Noor and Kit,” and the second with “Dear Anaïs,” on its own line. Alex can replace just Noor and Kit’s body with “We made your lemon pasta in the blue bowl on Tuesday. Thank you for both the bowl and the recipe.” Editing the shared note later leaves that personal body intact.

| Choice | Resolution | Approval rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shared note with names** | Compose the sender’s shared body and sign-off with each household’s confirmed greeting. Offer joint names, an explicitly chosen general greeting or an explicitly blank greeting. Display the exact shared body once, the complete list of resolved greetings and exceptions, and a full-card preview for any row. | One explicit **Approve shared note for N selected households** action. Show who is included. Resolve each row to an exact revision on the server. Missing or ambiguous names need the sender’s choice. No 100 identical body approvals. |
| **Personalize this card** | Start from that household’s resolved note. Edits to greeting, body or sign-off become field-specific overrides. Indicate which fields still use shared defaults. **Use shared wording again** shows the replacement and asks before discarding an edited body. | Review the complete customized note. Approving shared wording excludes customized bodies awaiting review. Moving between modes preserves existing personal notes. |
| **Help me draft** | Offer suggestions for one household or selected blank notes using its confirmed gift, supplied memory and chosen tone. Preserve imported or typed messages verbatim until the sender accepts an edit. Keep generated suggestions separate from sender text. | Each distinct generated message must be shown in full and accepted. No silent bulk approval of unseen AI text. Never invent attendance, relationships, gift use or cash amounts. An unavailable model leaves writing and named batch-send usable. |

The greeting resolver uses a confirmed inside name or literal greeting from that row. The envelope name, the personal return-address name and the signature are independently editable. A named salutation copied from the first recipient, unresolved placeholder or another household’s private detail blocks bulk approval until reviewed. Treat an intentional blank as different from a missing greeting. Changing shared text reopens only approvals inheriting that field. Changed recipient identity or greeting reopens the affected note. Changing only a street/unit triggers envelope review and preserves approved wording. Paid snapshots stay immutable.

**Blend with existing multi-send.** From an unpaid general batch offer **Continue as a thank-you project**. Show the count and the fields to be carried over. Copy design, handwriting, sign-off, return address, recipients, explicit greetings and shared body into a new owned project once. Preserve blank and custom greetings. Flag inherited named greetings for resolution. Leave the original draft recoverable. If it has a pending intent, settle or confirm cancellation before allowing the same batch to be purchased through the project. A paid order opens its status. A couple starting inside the wedding portal gets the same familiar shared-body interaction without a detour through general checkout.

## Project and checkout contracts

| Record or boundary | Proposed contract |
| --- | --- |
| Project and households | Project stores owner or guest capability hash, occasion, shared defaults, accepted cover reference, current revision and settled purchase count. Household child records have stable IDs, explicit greeting, overrides, approval fingerprint, address state and independent payment state. Use paginated child records. Reject stale writes with a conflict view that preserves edits. |
| Imports and artwork | Hash import plus mapping version for idempotent review/apply. Bound file bytes, expanded XLSX size, sheets, rows, model calls and processing time. Reject oversize input visibly before partial commit. Treat cells as data. Do not execute formulas/macros or fetch embedded links. Use minimal fields for any model call. Originals and previews are private. Reuse JPG/PNG/WebP image validation, metadata stripping and existing pixel/size limits. Pin the accepted master bytes, hash and revision to the batch before payment. |
| Ownership and lifetime | Authorize every read, preview, mutation, export and checkout server-side. Use a secure HttpOnly guest session, separate expiring single-use recovery capabilities and narrower address-request tokens. Rotate on recovery/claim, throttle retries and remove capability tokens from URLs after exchange. Deny direct client writes to these records. Proposed retention: delete raw list imports within seven days of confirmed extraction and expire abandoned drafts after 90 days with notice. Keep paid snapshots under the order policy. Asset cleanup must preserve active project masters through draft retention and paid order copies through order retention. |
| Quote and reservation | Server loads approved rows, accepted master, confirmed domestic addresses and previous settled count. Price the selected envelopes in integer cents, in stable frozen order. Persist the project, version, ordinal and per-card allocation. One active purchase reservation per project prevents duplicate rows or tier positions in competing tabs. Client quantities, prices and “previously purchased” input are never authoritative. |
| Payment and fulfillment | Version the wedding contract alongside existing single and multi-send v1 readers. Freeze greeting, body, sign-off, style, sender/recipient addresses, cover hash, approvals and price for every selected household. Complete a bounded, checksummed manifest before intent creation. Reuse the exact snapshot on retries. Fence superseded intents until cancellation or reconciliation. A late verified capture binds only to its own frozen snapshot. Commit the purchased count once on verified settlement. Refunds use the original allocation; paid ordinals stay consumed and supplier-funded replacements earn no new tier. |

## Release evidence

The implementation is done when the **hosted** staging URL returns 200, a tester can find the portal from the homepage, V1–V7 have evidence, and the validator can reconcile the 98+2 test from imported rows to paid snapshots. Record branch and SHA, App Hosting build/rollout and traffic revision, fixture IDs and counts, desktop/mobile captures, sanitized acceptance results, rule/index status and the environment’s payment and delivery modes. Do not record raw household records, tokens, upload URLs or payment credentials. Staging acceptance uses Stripe test mode and held fulfillment. Never run a live fulfillment script to obtain evidence.

**Rollback.** Disable new wedding starts and checkout via the feature flag. Retain authorized reads and reconciliation for existing paid snapshots, restore the prior staging release if needed, and rerun the original single/multi-send smoke checks. A failed import, broken cover return path, wrong-name approval or unreconciled charged amount blocks acceptance even if the route loads.

**Dependencies and decisions for review.** The executor needs staging private storage, Firestore rules/indexes, test Stripe access and configurable drafting/address adapters. Hosted smart intake must demonstrate an actual suggestion and a usable fallback with its provider mode identified. Synthetic postal responses establish workflow behavior only. Supplier volume price, physical text fit, throughput and production activation remain separate commercial and operational decisions. The proposed 250-draft limit, 100-card batch cap, project retention and pilot rates are explicit validator review points.
