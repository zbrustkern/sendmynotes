# Sprint 0030 design and acceptance review

## Interpretation

Verified retained orders determine observed repeat behavior. The gross amount spent acquiring each new buyer can break even by a chosen age when cumulative contribution per buyer at that age equals fully loaded CAC. The observed figure excludes fixed overhead and changes as refunds, disputes and costs are reconciled. With a small cohort, show the numerator, denominator and observation date. The founder recalls a family sender making two purchases without paid acquisition. This is a reconciliation lead. No independent repeat has been verified in this review.

Measurement already records first observed attribution, a session-bound touch and legacy attribution. The order-level coupon/source table shows applied positive discounts, Google paid attribution, overlap and unknown source across the full history. Commercial classification is a separate review decision. Purchase cohorts keep the first purchase's campaign when a later visit comes from a different source.

## Inputs for an actual comparison

- The existing private reconciliation manifest can classify an order as `friends_family`. An independent decision must reference a bound live USD capture. The source reads immutable revisions through the cutoff, prefers the dated provider capture, and accepts a reviewed capture date for older evidence. Commercial review happens before identity matching. Do not infer a family relationship from a name or coupon.
- The optional `repeatBuyer` object on manifest payment evidence supports a documented per-order `identityUseApproved` decision. Leave it unset for receipt-only or unreviewed records. A current withdrawal, redaction, unsynced or omitted email, restricted attribution, or account deletion request suppresses matching. The server derives the current version's HMAC from the effective checkout address on each read. It does not consult an enrollment key.
- Use `taxCents`, actual `processorFeeCents` or the bound capture's fee, a complete list of `supplierAllocationIds`, and `otherCostsCents` for variable costs incurred at purchase. The referenced invoice rows must be actual, USD and linked to that same order. A documented zero is a value. Later resends or other incremental costs use `laterCosts` with `incurredAt` and `cents`. Record attributable paid reacquisition separately in `reacquisitionCents` and omit it from acquisition-period spend. Document any reliable shared `projectId` privately.
- The source uses successful dated refunds. Reviewed `disputeResolutions` reference the ledger's dispute ID, final outcome, resolution date and net loss exclusive of funds already refunded. Taxable partial reversals require actual returned tax per provider object in `reversedTaxCents`. A missing date or uncertain outcome can withhold financial and retained-repeat metrics.
- Each reviewed campaign-month allocation lives under the existing private environment-specific reconciliation root in `campaignPeriods`. Use one row per campaign/month with `business: "sendmynotes"`, Chicago `month`, the existing measurement `campaignKey`, a reviewed non-personal `campaignLabel`, `reviewedAt` in milliseconds and explicit `spendIds` from the dated ledger. Include gross acquisition media spend, including non-converting clicks. Set `creativeAndPartnerCents` and `creditCents` separately. Mark `fullAcquisitionPeriod` true only after reviewing the entire acquisition and attribution window and reconciling other-brand and reacquisition spend. Unreviewed billing rows and ambiguous allocations do not support CAC.
- First-purchase cohorts and metrics use eligible records known by the cutoff. Review, import, refund confirmation and capture dates govern financial knowledge. Later-posted activity can restate an age using its effective date. Current privacy restrictions apply on every read. The API accepts an optional ISO UTC `asOf`. All-history coverage counts are labeled separately from the selected buyer cohort.

## Verification

The staged worked example uses four fabricated buyers, five orders, $45 net revenue, $25 actual variable costs and $80 acquisition spend. At 30 days it produces 1 of 4 repeats, 1.25 retained orders per buyer, $11.25 revenue per buyer, $5 contribution per buyer, $20 CAC and $15 unrecovered per buyer.

Two requested Luna agents worked on the calculator and independent synthetic fixture suite. The coordinator integrated private repository code, corrected horizon and ordering edge cases, and verified source and route behavior. The tests cover age boundaries, partial maturity, refunds, lost disputes, negative contribution, missing costs, identity exclusions, attribution overlap, same-time parent ordering, source truncation, auth, aggregate-only output, closed period spend, historical reads and family exclusion. Staging records and the worked example are separate, authenticated read-only views. Production classification, identity matching and spend reconciliation were not performed during this sprint.

## November 18 production attribution cross-check

At 6:05 p.m. Chicago time, a bounded read-only projection found nine eligible positive USD payment snapshots bound to distinct parent orders and payment intents. Five had Google paid attribution and four had another recorded source. One of those four had an applied coupon with a positive discount. None of the five Google-attributed orders had that coupon signal. None of the nine had an explicit independent or friends-and-family decision in the private current reconciliation records. These are order counts. Checkout contacts, names and addresses were excluded from the projection. The cross-check establishes neither a distinct buyer count nor a repeat rate.
